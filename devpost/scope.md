# Fine Print

Paste a Devpost hackathon link; get every rule that can disqualify you as a checklist, each item quoting the exact sentence it came from, plus the deadline in your own time zone.

## The Unique Kernel
**Receipts.** Every checklist item is pulled from the hackathon's own rules and shows the sentence it came from, highlighted, so you can trust it without re-reading 40,000 characters of legal text. Then it checks your repo against those rules for you.

## Who It's For
A first-time or solo hackathon entrant (like most of the 3,359 people registered for Build With AI: Basics) who skims the overview page, builds for a week, and only discovers at submission time that the video had to be under 3 minutes on YouTube, the repo needed an open-source licence, or three planning files were mandatory. Today they read the rules page themselves (long, legal, easy to skim past) or find out from a rejection.

## The Core Loop
Open Fine Print → paste a hackathon URL → see the deadline counting down in local time and a checklist of hard requirements with receipts → paste your GitHub repo to auto-check the repo rules → tick off the rest as you go. They come back before submitting, and for every new hackathon.

## Inspiration & Identity
The fine print itself: off-white paper, a confident serif headline, monospace rule quotes with a yellow highlighter mark, a red "DQ risk" stamp on anything not yet cleared. Calm, trustworthy, a little wry. Not a generic purple-gradient AI dashboard.

## Why This Matters to the Learner
Olly enters many hackathons at once (three deadlines in the same week); missing one buried rule wastes a week of work. It's a tool he'd use himself.

## What "Working" Looks Like
Paste https://learn-ai-basics.devpost.com/ → within seconds: "Deadline Mon 26 Oct, 10:00 pm your time · 12 days 6 hrs", and a checklist including "Video under 3 minutes, public on YouTube or Vimeo", "Public repo with an open-source licence", "Repo must include scope.md, prd.md, spec.md", each with its highlighted source sentence. Paste a GitHub repo → licence, public visibility and the three files tick themselves green. That auto-tick is the "oh, that's cool" beat.

## The POC Boundary
- Devpost hackathon URLs only (*.devpost.com).
- Extract: title, deadline (converted to local time + countdown), hard requirements with source quotes, judging criteria.
- Repo check against GitHub: public, licence detected, required files present, README present.
- Manual ticks remembered in the browser per hackathon.

## Later
Other platforms (Kaggle, Lablab, MLH); an LLM pass for unusual rules; email/calendar reminders; a browser extension on Devpost pages; tracking several hackathons on one dashboard.

## Explicitly Cut
- Accounts / login — the browser remembers ticks; nobody needs an account for a checklist.
- Submitting to Devpost for you — out of scope and against the spirit of entering yourself.
- Judging or scoring your project — we check rules, not quality.
